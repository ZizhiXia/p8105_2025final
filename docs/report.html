Final Report

Motivation

CitiBike is a key component of New York City’s public transportation network, providing an affordable and flexible mobility option for residents and visitors. Understanding how and when the system is used can reveal valuable insights into urban mobility patterns, commuter behavior, and the impact of external factors such as weather, time of day, and user demographics. This project aims to analyze trip-level data from CitiBike NYC to identify factors that influence ride frequency, distance, and station usage. The findings could inform operational decisions—such as bike rebalancing and station placement—to improve service availability and enhance the overall user experience.

Related Work

This project is methodologically and thematically grounded in the comprehensive analysis presented by Todd W. Schneider in his article, “A Tale of Twenty-Two Million Citi Bikes: Analyzing the NYC Bike Share System.” Schneider’s work stands as a foundational public analysis of the CitiBike system, processing and visualizing the system’s first two years of operation, encompassing 22 million individual trips. His approach provides a direct blueprint for our study. We draw particular inspiration from his rigorous data engineering process, which involved reconstructing station location histories to ensure accurate spatial mapping—a consideration critical for any longitudinal study of urban infrastructure. Furthermore, his exploratory analysis established key patterns that our project seeks to revisit and extend with more recent data; these include the identification of the most popular stations and routes, the stark difference in usage patterns between weekdays (dominated by short, commute-like trips) and weekends (characterized by longer, leisure-oriented rides), and the spatial clustering of trips within Manhattan’s core. By applying a similar analytical lens to data from 2023, our work inherently tests the persistence of these patterns over time and serves as a comparative update to his seminal findings. Schneider’s use of tools like R, PostGIS, and Leaflet for large-scale data processing and interactive visualization also informs our own technical strategy, reinforcing the value of open-source tools in civic data analysis. Thus, his article not only provides a substantive prior benchmark against which we can compare our results but also outlines a proven analytical framework for investigating the spatial-temporal dynamics of large-scale bike-share systems.

Initial Questions

Our initial questions include:

• How does ride frequency vary by time of day, day of the week, and season?
• What are the most popular start and end stations, and how do they differ by time or user type?
• How do factors such as user gender, age group, and membership type affect ride distance and duration?
• Are there identifiable commuter routes or leisure-oriented usage patterns?

Data

Data Source and Collection

The primary dataset used in this analysis is the official trip-level data provided by Citi Bike NYC, publicly available through their System Data portal. We downloaded all monthly trip files for the calendar year 2023 (January through December). These files were ingested and combined programmatically.

Raw Data Variables

Each row in the raw dataset represents a single bike trip, with the following key columns
(a subset of all available fields is listed here for relevance):

  • ride_id: A unique identifier for each trip
  • rideable_type: The type of bike used (classic_bike or electric_bike)
  • started_at, ended_at: The start and end timestamps of the trip
  • start_station_name, end_station_name: Station names
  • start_station_id, end_station_id: Station identifiers
  • start_lat, start_lng, end_lat, end_lng: Coordinates
  • member_casual: Membership status

Data Cleaning and Processing

We performed a multi-step cleaning and transformation process to prepare the data for analysis using R and the tidyverse suite of packages.

Resulting Cleaned Dataset

The final cleaned dataset served as the foundation for all subsequent analysis. Key analytical variables included the original station and member information, alongside the newly created temporal features (duration_min, month_lab, hour, is_weekend). This structured format allowed for flexible aggregation and visualization by time, space, and user type.

Exploratory Analysis

Spatial Patterns: Station Popularity

Our analysis revealed a high concentration of activity in Manhattan’s core areas.
Top findings include:

  • W 21 St & 6 Ave as the most popular start station (~140,000 rides)
  • Overlap between top start and end station lists
  • Strong clustering around Midtown, Central Park, and Hudson River Greenway

Interpretation:
This spatial convergence suggests these stations function as both major origins and destinations, indicating balanced usage. It also raises equity considerations due to low-density ridership in outer boroughs.

Temporal Patterns: Monthly and Seasonal Trends

Monthly ride counts showed:

  • Members dominate overall usage year-round
  • Casual riders show strong summer spikes (July–August)
  • Winter usage drops sharply for casual riders

Interpretation:
Members’ stability reflects commuting routines; casual riders show weather sensitivity.

User Behavioral Patterns: Weekday vs Weekend

Key findings:

  • Members show sharp commuting peaks at 8 AM and 6 PM on weekdays
  • Casual riders show smoother afternoon peaks
  • Both groups display flatter curves on weekends

Interpretation:
Confirms dual-purpose system: weekday commuting vs weekend leisure.

Ride Duration Analysis

Ride duration is right-skewed. Findings include:

  • Longer average durations in warm months (peak 16.8 min in May)
  • Shortest durations in December (12.9 min)
  • Longest durations between 12–4 AM (often >18 min)

Interpretation:
Nighttime rides may serve niche purposes—recreation, nightlife transport, or service industry commutes.

Route and Trip Purpose Analysis

Frequent origin-destination pairs show:

  • Central Park loops dominate (most common: Central Park S & 6 Ave → same station)
  • Roosevelt Island Tramway loops also strong
  • Short-distance commuting pairs in Financial District

Interpretation:
Recreational loops and commuting corridors coexist within the system.

Vehicle Type Preference

Both member and casual riders used classic and electric bikes in roughly equal proportion (~50/50).

Interpretation:
Bike type does not differentiate user behavior significantly.

Additional Analysis

Interactive Spatial Visualization

Two Leaflet maps were created:

Methodology:

  • Circle markers scaled by ride count
  • Viridis color gradients
  • Hover labels + click popups

Findings:
The maps confirmed:

  • Extremely dense Manhattan clusters
  • Symmetry between start and end station patterns
  • Very sparse distribution in outer boroughs except for a few hotspots

Comprehensive Station Ranking Analysis

Methodology:

  • Faceted bar chart comparing start, end, and total rides
  • Direct labels for ride counts

Findings:

  • Rankings consistent across all three metrics
  • Top stations exceed 100,000 annual rides
  • Roughly 10–15 stations dominate overall activity

Discussion

Summary of Key Findings

Our analysis reveals:

  • Strong spatial concentration in Manhattan
  • Clear temporal rhythms (seasonal + weekday/weekend + daily commute peaks)
  • Distinct behavior between members and casual riders
  • Mixture of trip purposes: commuting, recreation, nighttime niche use

Alignment with Initial Questions and Expectations

Findings matched expectations, but also introduced new insights:

  • Nighttime long-duration pattern stronger than anticipated
  • Bike-type neutrality was surprising
  • Updated quantitative station rankings add detail beyond prior work

Conclusion

This comprehensive analysis highlights CitiBike’s dual purpose as both a transportation utility and recreational amenity. It emphasizes spatial and temporal dynamics essential for system planning. The findings offer actionable insights for improving service efficiency and enhancing user experience, while also extending prior research with updated 2023 data.
